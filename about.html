our story

Rooted, and reaching outward.

Founded in Vancouver in 2025, by a poet who became a producer.

the founder

Kochar Abubaker

Kochar Abubaker is a Kurdish author, producer, and cultural activist based in Vancouver, where she founded Haniko Film Corp. in 2025. She advocates passionately for women's rights and has authored the autobiographical novel Nothingness and the award-winning poetry collection My Father's Pigeons.

Since moving to Canada in 2016, Kochar has remained a powerful voice for Kurdish women, championing their stories and struggles, and has supported refugee resettlement through Journey Home Community in British Columbia. Haniko carries that same conviction onto the screen: that neither poetry nor production has to be diluted for the other.

"The most powerful bridges between cultures are built one honest story at a time."

Kochar Abubaker

why we exist

Our vision

Haniko exists to carry Kurdish stories into the world without asking them to shrink to fit, and to bring the world's stories home to Kurdish audiences with the same respect.

While the focus is Kurdish film, Haniko is equally open to international cinematic work, met with the same care and respect.

how we work toward it

Our mission

Produce

Documentary and narrative work centered on Kurdish life, land, and language, without exoticizing or over-explaining.

Bridge

Actively seek co-productions and cross-border storytelling partnerships, rather than working in isolation.

Elevate

Treat Kurdish as a cinematic language in its own right, subtitled and scored with the same rigor as any major festival entry.

Mentor

Support the next generation of Kurdish and diaspora filmmakers, especially women, as Haniko grows.

what we hold to

Values

RootednessKurdish language, land, and memory are treated as subject matter worth full craft, not backdrop.
Bridge-buildingEvery slate includes at least one project pairing Kurdish stories with another culture or tradition.
Poetic rigorVisual and narrative choices favor precision and restraint: trust the image, trust the silence.
Access without dilutionSubtitled and contextualized for international audiences, without simplifying Kurdish specificity.
IndependenceSmall and founder-led by design, able to take on stories a larger studio might consider too specific.

Stories from Kurdistan and beyond, told with the discipline of poetry and the reach of cinema.
